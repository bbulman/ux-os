## 4. Brand voice

### The voice in one line

**Expert-yet-vulnerable.** Someone who has done this work for fifteen years, speaks with earned authority, and is also honest about the moments they got it wrong.

### Voice pillars

**Practitioner, not pundit.** Every claim is grounded in a real session, a real client, or a real mistake. Abstractions get cashed out in specifics. "Ideation can stall" becomes "Twenty minutes in, the Director of Marketing has stopped making eye contact with the sticky notes."

**Warm but not soft.** Direct, plainspoken, occasionally funny. Never twee. Never corporate-empathetic ("We know how hard this can be!"). The warmth comes from accuracy, from understanding what the reader is actually going through, not from performed kindness.

**Vulnerable as a feature, not a bit.** Personal stories appear sparingly and only when they teach something. "I've been there" is not the point. "Here's the specific thing I learned when I was there" is the point.

**Inclusive as a default.** Mobility, neurodivergence, seniority, remote and hybrid, introverts and extroverts. These aren't DEI bullet points. They're baked into the design of exercises, UI copy, and examples. If an instruction assumes everyone can stand up, it's wrong.

### What the voice is not

- LinkedIn broetry. ("I almost gave up on facilitation. Then one thing changed everything.")
- Breathless positivity. ("Workshops are MAGIC ✨.")
- Jargon-soaked consultant-speak. ("Alignment catalysts." "Innovation accelerants.")
- Hedging, qualified academic prose.
- LLM-default register. (DESIGN.md §6 lists the tells.)

## 5. Writing style

### Sentence-level

- Short sentences are fine. Fragments are fine. Use them when they punch.
- Active voice by default. Passive only when the receiver of the action is genuinely the subject.
- Concrete nouns over abstract nouns. "The Director" beats "the stakeholder." "A sticky note" beats "an input artifact."
- Numbers for time and counts. "Three exercises, 45 minutes" beats "a handful of exercises over the better part of an hour."
- Contractions always. (it's, you'll, don't.) Formal register is a red flag.

### Punctuation

- **Em dashes:** fine, but not in every sentence. If em dashes appear in more than two of every five sentences, edit them out.
- **Oxford comma:** yes.
- **Exclamation points:** almost never. One per article, tops, and only for genuine surprise.
- **Semicolons:** sparingly; used when two full thoughts are closer than a period would suggest.

### Hard banned words and phrases (a sample)

These are AI tells, consultant-speak, or both. They get edited out aggressively:

- delve, delves, delving
- navigate (as a metaphor, as in "navigate complexity")
- leverage (as a verb)
- unleash, unlock (as a verb for potential, power, etc.)
- game-changer, game-changing
- in today's fast-paced world
- robust, seamless, cutting-edge
- journey (as a metaphor for any process)
- "not just X, but Y" (one per piece, max)
