## ⭐ PRIMARY: Maya Okonkwo, The Working Facilitator

> *"My clients never see the workshop. They see the agenda that lands before it and the recap that lands after. Those two documents are my reputation."*

*[Section left out: personal details, technology comfort, social media and Workshopr footprint.]*

### Day in the life

**Morning.** Delivery days start at 6:00. She re-reads the run-of-show she exported from Planner, checks her timing buffers one last time, and packs materials against the agenda's materials list. On site by 8:15 to reset the room: boards up, markers tested, Facilitator open on the laptop before the first participant arrives at 9:00.

**Afternoon.** She delivers. Timers, scripts and her own notes live on the laptop screen; her attention lives in the room. She captures parking-lot items as they surface and photographs every flip chart before the room resets at 3:30. On non-delivery days the afternoon is agenda work: assembling next week's session from the library, running it through Agenda Check, and taking discovery calls for new engagements.

**Evening.** The recap. Board export and photos go into Synthesize; she spends ten minutes editing themes into her own voice, fixes an owner on two action items, and sends the client summary before 7:00 while the session energy is still warm. Then a chapter of a facilitation ebook, and prep for tomorrow.

### Design implications

- Speed-to-draft is sacred in every phase: agenda assembly, pre-flight, synthesis. Protect it from creeping friction.
- Provenance is non-negotiable. She will not send a client anything she cannot trace.
- Editing must feel like authoring. The AI drafts; she finishes in her voice.
- She is the lifecycle persona: Designed, then Delivered, then Synthesized in one connected object is her literal work week. The north-star metric counts her.
