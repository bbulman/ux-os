## The core job

> **When** I'm the one responsible for a workshop,
> **I want to** turn limited prep time into a session that runs well and outcomes that stick,
> **so I can** prove the room's time was well spent, and mine too.

Nobody wakes up wanting a facilitation platform. They wake up owning a room full of expensive people and a result they'll be judged on. The job is **"make the workshop worth everyone's time, then prove it."** This framing explains the whole persona set at once: Maya is judged by clients, Devin by his team, Priya by her own nerve, Marcus by a CFO. It also explains the north-star metric: a workshop only fully "happened" when it was designed, delivered and synthesized, because until the outcomes stick, the job isn't done.

### The three dimensions

| Dimension | The job | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional** | Design a credible agenda fast; run the room on time with everyone heard; convert raw output into decisions, actions and a recap | Blank page to credible agenda in under an hour; session ends on time; recap out same day; actions land where work lives |
| **Emotional** | Walk in certain the plan holds; stay present with people instead of juggling logistics; end the day without dreading the writeup | Pre-room calm; in-room flow; evenings back |
| **Social** | Look like a professional to the room; be known as the person whose sessions actually change things | The client forwards the recap; the team stops dreading retros; the CFO nods |

## The job map (Ulwick's eight stages)

| Stage | What's happening | Outcome expectation | Workshopr today | Underserved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Define** | Frame what the session must achieve, for whom, in how long | Be certain a workshop is the right tool for the goal | Strategist; goal field in Planner | The strategy step is hidden; most users skip straight to the agenda |
| **2 · Locate** | Find trusted building blocks | Minimize time to an exercise that fits the time, group and energy | Library (exercises, icebreakers, ready-made workshops) | Fit signals are thin: what's safe for beginners, what works for this group size |
| **3 · Prepare** | Assemble agenda, timings, materials | Minimize blank-page-to-credible-agenda time | Planner, ready-made workshops, materials lists | Brand polish is manual; no client-facing share view |
| **4 · Confirm** | Validate the plan before the room does | Maximize confidence the plan survives contact with the room | Agenda Check | First-timer confidence framing; rehearsal support |
| **5 · Execute** | Run the session | Stay present with the room, not the plan | Facilitator, Sidekick, Intervention cards, live layer | Capture competes with facilitating; needs one-tap capture |
| **6 · Monitor** | Track time, energy, participation | Know it's drifting before it's lost | Timers, live reactions, polls, help requests | Energy is felt, not measured; no drift warning |
| **7 · Modify** | Adapt mid-flight, rescue stalls | Recover without the room noticing | Run-mode item controls; Intervention cards | No re-flow math: what to cut to still end on time |
| **8 · Conclude** | Synthesize, recap, assign, follow up | Outcomes leave the room and stick | Synthesize, Workshop Record | **The big one: impact evidence, commitment tracking, integrations** |

The map makes the platform's shape legible: stages 2-5 are well served (that's the mature core), stages 1 and 8 are the frontier, and 6-7 are served for time but not for energy.
